# Figure captions

Figure 2 | Observed acquisition hierarchy and paired uncertainty under grade-matched negatives. (A) On 43 eligible DeepDRiD test patients with explicit L1/L2/R1/R2 images, each small dot is a patient-level cosine similarity for same-eye different-view, cross-eye same-patient, or grade-matched cross-patient comparison. Cross-patient values are first averaged over the five fixed negative assignments within each patient; diamonds mark group means. (B) Same-eye minus cross-eye and cross-eye minus grade-matched cross-patient differences on test (43 patients) and validation (45 patients). Thin intervals are separate 95% paired-patient bootstrap confidence intervals from 3,000 resamples for each of five fixed negative seeds; colored diamonds are across-seed mean differences and do not carry a pooled confidence interval. The same-eye minus cross-eye intervals include zero, so their mean ordering is not established. All displayed cross-eye minus matched-negative intervals are above zero. These comparisons do not control image quality and therefore cannot establish a disease- and quality-independent identity factor.

Figure 3 | DeepDRiD pair correspondence under progressively stricter negative-patient controls. (A) Full-cohort diagnostic analysis of 300 complete bilateral patients. Gray trajectories show five fixed negative-pair assignments; teal points and whiskers show mean ± sample standard deviation (SD) across those seeds. Offset gray whiskers show the conservative envelope from the minimum lower to the maximum upper bound of five separately computed 95% paired-patient bootstrap confidence intervals (3,000 resamples per seed); this envelope is not itself a pooled confidence interval. The AUROC axis is enlarged and does not include chance level 0.5. (B) Four left–right view combinations on 43 eligible held-out test patients, with DR-grade-matched negatives; whiskers are across-seed SD. (C) Mean absolute source–negative difference in a dimensionless, normalized quality-composite score (overall quality, clarity, field definition and inverse artifact score); lower means tighter matching. (D) Training-fitted linear probes evaluated on 44 test patients. DR is diabetic retinopathy; AUROC is area under the receiver operating characteristic curve; OvR is one-versus-rest. Probe performance indicates readable metadata, not causal leakage. Panel A is not a held-out learned-model result.

Figure 4 | Frozen external baseline and learned-candidate transfer boundary. (A) Unmodified PAPILA fundus photographs from illustrative patient RET014; OS denotes left eye and OD right eye. The example was chosen by identifier, not by score. (B) On 244 complete bilateral PAPILA patients, fixed ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 features yield pair-identity AUROC for random and exact bilateral glaucoma-diagnosis-signature-matched cross-patient negatives. Dots show five fixed negative-pair seeds; points and whiskers are their mean ± sample SD. No model, threshold, or negative rule was tuned on PAPILA. (C) On previously inspected CFP–OCT development data (137 eligible patients), the low-capacity hierarchical H-CBFD candidate scores below raw frozen features under both prespecified diabetic-macular-edema (DME) label schemas. Patient-max uses the maximum eye-level label per patient; eye-mode is the legacy patient-mode convention from eye-level labels. Points and whiskers show mean ± sample SD over five negative-pair seeds. Differences are H-CBFD minus raw AUROC. H-CBFD was not evaluated on PAPILA, so panel B does not validate its external generalization.
