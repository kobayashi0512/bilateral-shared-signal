Figure 2. Four-view hierarchy under exact bilateral disease and target-eye quality matching. (A) Identity-free patient-level cosine scores for same-eye repeat views, same-patient cross-eye views, and assigned cross-patient negatives in the 33-patient held-out test subset. Diamonds and colored whiskers show group means and patient-node bootstrap 95% intervals; the negative interval is the envelope across five fixed assignments. (B) Same-eye minus cross-eye and cross-eye minus matched-negative contrasts in validation (n=35) and test (n=33). Points are means across five assignments; whiskers span the smallest lower and largest upper endpoints of assignment-specific patient-node bootstrap intervals. They are not pooled confidence intervals. Exact negative matching uses the ordered left/right diabetic-retinopathy grade signature and right-eye quality-composite tier; the anchor left-eye image is fixed within each positive/negative comparison. The same-eye versus cross-eye difference remains unresolved, whereas cross-eye scores exceed matched negatives in both splits. All intervals are conditional on fixed assignments and do not include rematching uncertainty.

Figure 3. Conditional correspondence under patient-level and stricter eye-specific negative controls. (A) Full-cohort descriptive diagnostic: three original patient-level controls in 300 patients and two post-hoc controls in 293 patients using the exact ordered bilateral diabetic-retinopathy signature, with or without exact right-eye quality-tier matching. (B) Held-out sensitivity analyses: the three original rules share a 42-patient denominator; stricter eye-specific rules share 34 patients. Colored dots are the five fixed assignment-specific AUROCs; diamonds and bold whiskers show mean ± sample SD across assignments; thin offset gray whiskers show the envelope of five separate 95% patient-node bootstrap intervals, not a pooled interval. The horizontal dashed line marks chance AUROC. The full-cohort analysis is descriptive, and the held-out controls were added post hoc. Quality aggregation differs between the original patient-level controls and the stricter target-eye rule; cross-condition changes are not causal estimates.
